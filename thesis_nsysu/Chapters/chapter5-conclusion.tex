\chapter{Conclusion}

\section{Summary}
This thesis investigates the use of a metasurface transmitarray lens antenna for communication channel evaluation. The theoretical background covers the metasurface transmitarray concept, antenna performance parameters, and communication channel evaluation. The simulation setup describes the unit cell design, transmitarray lens configuration, ANSYS HFSS simulation, and channel evaluation workflow.

\section{Conclusion}
The simulation and evaluation framework provides a way to connect antenna-level electromagnetic performance with communication-level channel behavior. The metasurface transmitarray lens antenna is evaluated through parameters such as transmission response, radiation pattern, gain, directivity, received power, path loss, and signal-to-noise ratio. These results are used to determine how the antenna design affects the communication link performance.

\section{Main Contributions}
The main contributions of this thesis can be summarized as follows:

\begin{itemize}
	\item The theoretical basis of metasurface transmitarray lens antennas and communication channel evaluation is reviewed.
	\item A simulation workflow for the metasurface transmitarray lens antenna is defined using ANSYS HFSS.
	\item The antenna simulation results are connected with channel-level performance metrics.
	\item The communication channel is evaluated based on link-related parameters such as received power, path loss, signal-to-noise ratio, and coverage.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
The evaluation is limited by the assumptions used in the antenna simulation and communication channel scenario. Material properties, fabrication tolerance, antenna alignment, environmental simplification, and propagation assumptions may affect the final results. Therefore, further validation is required when measurement data or a more detailed propagation environment becomes available.

\section{Future Work}
Future work may include measurement validation of the fabricated transmitarray lens antenna, more detailed channel evaluation in realistic environments, comparison between ray tracing and hybrid electromagnetic approaches, and optimization of the antenna design for improved link performance.
